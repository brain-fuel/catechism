# Guide to the Divine Service

The Divine Service is God’s service to his people through Word and Sacrament,
and the congregation’s answering service of faith, praise, and prayer. This
original guide follows the historic *Common Service* without reproducing its
layout or distinctive wording.

## Preparation

Invocation, confession, and absolution place worshippers before the Triune
God. Their biblical ground is Matthew 28:19, Psalm 32, and John 20:22–23;
Svebilius’s teaching on Baptism and absolution explains the gifts. The
congregation stands, confesses, and receives forgiveness. Daily life continues
this return to Baptism through contrition and faith.

## Service of the Word

Introit, Kyrie, Gloria, collect, readings, creed, hymn, sermon, and prayer let
God speak and the Church answer. Their pattern appears in the Psalms, Luke
2:14, 1 Timothy 4:13, the apostolic creeds, and 1 Timothy 2:1. Svebilius joins
Scripture, Creed, and Prayer as one school of faith. Listen, confess, sing, and
intercede; then carry the heard Word into vocation.

## Service of the Sacrament and Distribution

Preface, Sanctus, Lord’s Prayer, Words of Institution, Pax, Agnus Dei,
distribution, and thanksgiving center upon Christ’s testament (Matthew
26:26–29; 1 Corinthians 10:16–17; 11:23–29). Svebilius confesses Christ’s true
body and blood, given for forgiveness. Approach repentant and trusting his
words; receive, eat, drink, and depart in peace. Reconciliation and mercy
toward neighbours flow from the shared gift.

## Closing and Benediction

Thanksgiving, dismissal, and the Aaronic blessing (Numbers 6:24–26) send the
baptized into their callings. The Table of Duties names those callings. Stand
to receive the blessing, answer Amen, and go in the peace bestowed by God’s
name.

## Stories, art, and sources

Isaiah’s temple vision (Isaiah 6:1–8) moves from holiness through absolution to
sending; the Emmaus disciples hear the Scriptures and know Christ in the
breaking of bread (Luke 24:13–35). Together these accounts trace the movement
from God’s gracious presence and spoken Word to forgiveness, Communion, and
the Christian’s return to daily vocation.
