Guide to the Divine Service

The Divine Service is God’s service to His people through Word and Sacrament, and the congregation’s answering service of faith, praise, prayer, and love. Its parts are not disconnected religious exercises. They form one movement: God gathers sinners, speaks His Word, gives Christ’s body and blood, and sends His forgiven people into their callings.

This guide follows the historic Lutheran Common Service. Local settings may place some canticles or prayers differently, but their biblical substance and purpose remain the same.

Preparation

The congregation enters quietly, remembers Holy Baptism, and prepares to hear God’s Word. In the Invocation, the Divine Name placed upon us in Baptism gathers us before the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost. Confession speaks the truth about our sin; absolution speaks Christ’s forgiveness to repentant sinners.

Matthew 28:19

Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost.

John 20:22–23

Receive ye the Holy Ghost: Whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted unto them; and whose soever sins ye retain, they are retained.

Svebilius’s teaching on Baptism, repentance, confession, and absolution explains this preparation. The congregation may kneel, confess aloud, and receive the pastor’s absolution as Christ’s own forgiving Word. Daily repentance continues this return to Baptism.

The Service of the Word

Introit (Entrance Antiphon)

The Introit is a Psalm or other biblical antiphon sung as the ministers enter. It announces the day’s theme and teaches that the Church approaches God through the words He has given.

Psalm 100:4

Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name.

Meaning: God gathers His people around His presence and promise. The entrance is therefore proclamation before it is ceremony.

Catechism: The Third Commandment directs Christians to hold God’s Word sacred and gladly hear and learn it. The Psalms give the congregation words of faith, lament, thanksgiving, and praise.

Congregation: Stand and sing or hear the antiphon attentively. Let its words introduce the readings and teaching appointed for the day.

Daily life: Begin prayer with God’s Word rather than waiting for a suitable mood. A Psalm can give faithful language to joy, fear, grief, or thanksgiving.

Kyrie (Lord Have Mercy)

The Kyrie is the congregation’s repeated cry, “Lord, have mercy.” It is not a second confession of sins, but the baptized people’s prayer for mercy upon the Church and the whole world.

Mark 10:47

Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me.

Meaning: Christian worship never outgrows dependence upon Christ’s mercy. The cry is confident because it is addressed to the merciful Lord who has drawn near.

Catechism: The petitions of the Lord’s Prayer teach us to bring every spiritual and bodily need to our Father. The Fifth Petition asks forgiveness; the Seventh asks deliverance from evil.

Congregation: Sing the Kyrie for oneself, one’s neighbour, the Church, and all who suffer.

Daily life: “Lord, have mercy” is a complete prayer in danger, confusion, or pain when longer words fail.

Gloria in Excelsis (Glory Be to God on High)

The Gloria joins the angels’ song at Christ’s birth with praise of the Father, the Lamb of God, and the Holy Ghost.

Luke 2:14

Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men.

Meaning: Mercy flowers into praise. The congregation rejoices that the eternal Son has come in the flesh to reconcile sinners to God.

Catechism: The Second Article confesses Jesus Christ, true God and true man, who redeemed us that we might be His own. The First Article teaches thanksgiving for the Father’s gifts; the Third Article confesses the Holy Ghost’s sanctifying work.

Congregation: Stand and sing as people who have received peace from God, attending especially to the Gloria’s confession of Christ.

Daily life: Praise names God’s works even when circumstances are painful. Christ’s incarnation and mercy remain true before our feelings change.

Collect (Opening Prayer)

The Collect gathers the congregation’s petitions into a brief prayer addressed to the Father through the Son, ordinarily concluding with a confession of the Holy Trinity.

John 16:23

Whatsoever ye shall ask the Father in my name, he will give it you.

Meaning: The prayer “collects” the theme of the day and asks that God’s Word bear fruit among those who hear it.

Catechism: The Introduction to the Lord’s Prayer teaches us to pray to God as dear children ask their dear father. Its petitions order prayer according to God’s name, kingdom, will, provision, forgiveness, protection, and deliverance.

Congregation: Join the prayer in attentive silence and make it one’s own with “Amen.”

Daily life: Brief, ordered prayer keeps daily devotion from becoming only a list of immediate anxieties.

Epistle Reading

The Epistle is read from the apostolic writings, though on some days another non-Gospel reading may be appointed. Through it Christ’s apostles teach His Church.

1 Timothy 4:13

Give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to doctrine.

Meaning: The risen Christ continues to instruct His people through the prophetic and apostolic Scriptures.

Catechism: Svebilius repeatedly grounds Christian doctrine in the written Word. The Third Article teaches that the Holy Ghost calls, enlightens, and sanctifies through the Gospel.

Congregation: Sit and listen for the reading’s teaching, warning, comfort, and connection to Christ.

Daily life: Read Scripture in context, not as isolated slogans. Receive apostolic correction as a gift meant to preserve faith and love.

Gradual (including Alleluia or Tract)

The Gradual is Scripture sung between the readings. “Hallelujah” means “Praise Yah.” During penitential seasons a Tract of Psalm verses may replace the Hallelujah.

Psalm 117:1–2

117:1 O praise Yahweh, all ye nations: praise him, all ye people.

117:2 For his merciful kindness is great toward us: and the truth of Yahweh endureth for ever. Hallelujah.

Meaning: The congregation answers one reading with Scripture and prepares to hear the Gospel. Joyful acclamation and penitential restraint both serve the proclamation of Christ.

Catechism: The Second Commandment teaches the holy use of God’s name in prayer, praise, and thanksgiving. The Third Commandment teaches glad hearing of His Word.

Congregation: Sing the appointed verses. Stand for the Hallelujah and Gospel according to local custom.

Daily life: Let one portion of Scripture lead to another. Praise and repentance need not compete; both arise from hearing God truthfully.

Gospel Reading

The Holy Gospel recounts the words and deeds of Jesus Christ. The congregation stands to receive it with particular honour, not because the other Scriptures are less inspired, but because the Gospel places Christ’s earthly words and works immediately before us.

John 20:31

These are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name.

Meaning: Christ is the center and fulfillment of Holy Scripture. His teaching, cross, and resurrection create and sustain faith.

Catechism: The Second Article confesses who Jesus is and what He has done. Every chief part finds its fulfillment and gift in Him.

Congregation: Stand, hear, and answer with praise to Christ. Attend to what Jesus says, does, promises, commands, suffers, and gives.

Daily life: Return to the Gospels often. Christian decisions and hopes are formed by the actual Christ revealed there, not by an imagined substitute.

Nicene Creed

The Nicene Creed is the Church’s common confession of the Triune God and of Christ’s saving incarnation, death, resurrection, ascension, and return.

Romans 10:9

If thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.

Meaning: The Creed answers God’s Word with the faith His Word creates. It also distinguishes the apostolic faith from teachings that deny the Holy Trinity or Christ’s person and work.

Catechism: It unfolds the same three articles taught in the Apostles’ Creed: creation, redemption, and sanctification.

Congregation: Stand and confess with the whole Church. “I believe” is both personal trust and participation in a confession received from those before us.

Daily life: Use the Creed to test religious claims and to remember whose work—not our achievement—creates, redeems, and sanctifies us.

Sermon

The sermon publicly explains and applies the appointed Scriptures, proclaiming Law and Gospel with Christ at the center.

2 Timothy 4:2

Preach the Word; be instant in season, out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort with all longsuffering and doctrine.

Meaning: Through preaching, the Law exposes sin and the Gospel delivers Christ’s forgiveness and life to hearers.

Catechism: Svebilius teaches doctrine by questions, answers, and biblical testimony. The Third Commandment requires not mere attendance but the glad hearing and learning of God’s Word.

Congregation: Sit, listen, examine the preaching by Scripture, repent where the Law accuses, and trust the promise of Christ.

Daily life: Carry one clear word of correction or comfort into the week. Discuss the sermon in the household without turning it into criticism of other hearers.

General Prayer (Prayer of the Church)

The General Prayer intercedes for the Church, civil authorities, homes, workers, the afflicted, and all people according to their needs.

1 Timothy 2:1–2

I exhort therefore, that, first of all, supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks, be made for all men; for kings, and for all that are in authority; that we may lead a quiet and peaceable life in all godliness and honesty.

Meaning: The congregation’s prayer widens beyond private concerns. Those gathered before God bear their neighbours’ needs in love.

Catechism: The Lord’s Prayer contains every necessary petition. The Table of Duties identifies the neighbours, offices, and authorities for whom Christians pray and whom they serve.

Congregation: Pray each petition actively and answer “Amen.” Names and particular needs may be included without turning prayer into announcement.

Daily life: Keep a household prayer list that includes congregation, government, enemies, those who suffer, and those whose work sustains common life.

The Service of the Sacrament

Preface and Sanctus (Holy, Holy, Holy)

The Preface begins the Church’s great thanksgiving: “Lift up your hearts” and “Let us give thanks unto the Lord our God.” The Sanctus joins Isaiah’s vision and Jerusalem’s acclamation of Jesus.

Isaiah 6:3

Holy, holy, holy, is Yahweh of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory.

Matthew 21:9

Hosanna to the Son of David: Blessed is he that cometh in the name of Yahweh; Hosanna in the highest.

Meaning: Heaven and earth meet around the Holy One who comes to save. The Church approaches neither an absent memory nor a common meal, but Christ’s testament.

Catechism: The Second Article identifies the coming Lord as our Redeemer. The explanation of The Lord’s Supper teaches us to trust Christ’s words and promise.

Congregation: Stand and sing with reverence and joy, giving thanks to the Father through Christ.

Daily life: Thanksgiving lifts the heart away from self-absorption and toward God’s gifts, while holiness teaches us not to treat those gifts casually.

Institution Narrative (Words of Institution)

The pastor speaks Christ’s own words over the bread and wine. These words identify what is given and state its purpose: Christ’s body and blood for the forgiveness of sins.

1 Corinthians 11:23–26

The Lord Jesus the same night in which he was betrayed took bread: and when he had given thanks, he brake it, and said, Take, eat: this is my body, which is broken for you: this do in remembrance of me. After the same manner also he took the cup, when he had supped, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood: this do ye, as oft as ye drink it, in remembrance of me. For as often as ye eat this bread, and drink this cup, ye do shew the Lord’s death till he come.

Meaning: The Sacrament rests upon Christ’s institution, not upon human imagination or intensity. His Word makes the gift and gives the gift’s promise.

Catechism: “Given and shed for you for the remission of sins” is the chief thing in the Sacrament. Faith receives what Christ’s words declare.

Congregation: Listen to the Words of Institution as Christ’s testament addressed to His Church. Fix faith upon “for you.”

Daily life: When conscience asks whether God is gracious, answer with Christ’s external promise rather than searching the strength of one’s feelings.

Agnus Dei (Lamb of God)

The Agnus Dei addresses Jesus as the Lamb who bears the world’s sin and asks Him for mercy and peace as the congregation approaches His Supper.

John 1:29

Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.

Meaning: The crucified and risen Lamb is both sacrifice and host. The peace sought here is the forgiveness He won upon the cross.

Catechism: The Second Article confesses redemption by Christ’s holy, precious blood. The Sacrament distributes the testament of that redemption.

Congregation: Sing toward Christ in humble confidence, then approach the altar as instructed.

Daily life: Christ’s peace frees Christians to confess sin honestly and to seek peace with neighbours without pretending that sin is harmless.

Distribution (Communion)

The baptized and instructed congregation receives Christ’s true body and blood under bread and wine, according to His institution.

1 Corinthians 10:16–17

The cup of blessing which we bless, is it not the communion of the blood of Christ? The bread which we break, is it not the communion of the body of Christ? For we being many are one bread, and one body: for we are all partakers of that one bread.

Meaning: Christ gives forgiveness, life, and salvation personally while joining communicants in one confession and fellowship.

Catechism: Worthiness does not consist in fasting or bodily preparation alone. The truly worthy guest has faith in Christ’s words, “given and shed for you for the remission of sins.”

Congregation: Receive according to the congregation’s order, hear the words spoken to you, eat and drink, and depart in peace. Those not communing may use the time for prayer and reflection.

Daily life: The shared gift calls communicants away from grudges, self-exaltation, and neglect of fellow members toward reconciliation and concrete mercy.

Nunc Dimittis (Song of Simeon)

After receiving Christ’s body and blood, the congregation may sing Simeon’s song: “Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace.”

Luke 2:29–32

Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace, according to thy Word: for mine eyes have seen thy salvation, which thou hast prepared before the face of all people; a light to lighten the Gentiles, and the glory of thy people Israel.

Meaning: Like Simeon holding the infant Christ, communicants have received the promised Savior and may face both daily departure and death in peace.

Catechism: The Second Article confesses Christ as Savior; the Seventh Petition asks for a blessed end; The Lord’s Supper strengthens faith in His promise.

Congregation: Stand and sing as thanksgiving and confession. “Depart” means both leaving the altar in peace and being ready, in Christ, for life’s final departure.

Daily life: Use the Nunc Dimittis at evening prayer, in sickness, and when fear of death presses upon the conscience.

Post-Communion Collect

The Post-Communion Collect thanks God for the salutary gift and asks that it strengthen faith toward Him and fervent love toward one another.

Psalm 107:1

O give thanks unto Yahweh, for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever.

Meaning: Thanksgiving does not complete or improve the Sacrament. It confesses that the gift was received and asks for its fruit in Christian life.

Catechism: Faith receives forgiveness from Christ; love then serves the neighbour. The Table of Duties describes the callings in which that love takes shape.

Congregation: Pray and answer “Amen,” asking that the received gift not be treated as an isolated moment.

Daily life: Give thanks after receiving God’s gifts, then ask whom those gifts equip you to forgive, protect, encourage, or serve.

The Sending

Benediction (Apostolic Greeting)

The Benediction places God’s name and peace upon the congregation before it returns to the world. The historic Lutheran service ordinarily uses the Aaronic blessing; an apostolic blessing may also be used.

Numbers 6:24–26

Yahweh bless thee, and keep thee: Yahweh make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: Yahweh lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace.

2 Corinthians 13:14

The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Ghost, be with you all. Amen.

Meaning: The blessing is not a wish that the congregation bless itself. God places His gracious name upon His people and sends them with bestowed peace.

Catechism: The Second Commandment teaches the right use of God’s name; Baptism places the Triune Name upon the Christian; the Table of Duties directs the blessed into their callings.

Congregation: Stand and receive the blessing with open ears, then answer “Amen.”

Daily life: Leave as one sent by God. His blessing accompanies ordinary work, family care, civic duty, suffering, witness, and rest.

The Whole Movement

Isaiah’s temple vision moves from God’s holiness through absolution to sending (Isaiah 6:1–8). The disciples on the Emmaus road hear the Scriptures, recognize Christ in the breaking of bread, and return to bear witness (Luke 24:13–35). The same pattern shapes the Divine Service:

  1. God gathers sinners in His name.
  2. God speaks His Law and Gospel.
  3. The Church confesses the faith and prays for the world.
  4. Christ gives His body and blood for forgiveness.
  5. God blesses and sends His people to serve their neighbours.
